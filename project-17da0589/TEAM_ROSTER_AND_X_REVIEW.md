### 2.3 What in IT³ *is* correct (pure arithmetic)

His latest preprint (Zenodo 23018681) labels some claims "Level A" (pure mathematics). I checked those I could, and
they hold:

- the seven quadratic subfields of ℚ(√2,√3,√5) have discriminants multiplying to 2¹⁶·3⁴·5⁴
  (`it3_seven_discriminants`);
- 57600 = 240² = 0xE100 is its square root (`it3_covolume`);
- 172 = 43 ≪ 2 (`it3_closure_shift`);
- 86 + 87 = 173 = 0xAD, with no carry between the two hex digits and low hex digit 13 (`it3_central_edge_hex`);
- 43 is the first prime Heegner number that isn't an Ogg prime (`it3_first_ogg_breaking_heegner`).

**One caveat:** "carry-free" holds only digit-by-digit in hexadecimal. In binary, 86 + 87 *does* carry
(`it3_central_edge_binary_carries`).

These facts are true, but true number facts don't make the physics true. The step from "173 = 0xAD" to "the vacuum is a
16-bit processor" is the part nobody has shown.

### 2.4 How this affects ORION

1. **No direct link found.** Nothing I could see on his account, his Zenodo records or the TGSATE blog (searched for
   "Logvinovich") mentions ORION, TGSATE or this team. If he is a collaborator, that relationship isn't in public view.
2. **Overlapping vocabulary is the main risk.** IT³ uses "topological friction", a "Topological Friction Index",
   toroidal geometry, compilers and registers. Those words are close to ORION's friction framework
   (`FRICTION_EXAMINATION.md`) and the "toroidal temporal translator" material. If IT³ text is pasted into ORION
   documents, readers may take ORION as endorsing IT³'s physics claims, which have open published defects (§2.2).
   **Suggestion:** if ORION uses any of his terms, give them ORION's own definitions and cite IT³ as an outside
   source, not as established physics.
3. **Reach.** His account has about 24K followers, far more than most of the team. A public endorsement from him
   would bring attention, and also bring the critiques in §2.2 to ORION's door.
4. **Where he fits best: the Proofs Arena.** IT³ already sorts its claims into Level A (pure math), Level B
   (postulates) and Level C (resonances). That matches the Arena's "proved vs. interpretive" tagging. Level A claims
   can be machine-checked, as done here. Level B and C claims need data tests built like Watts's protocol (frozen
   operators, null catalogues, a holdout), not proofs.
5. **@Voltardark (Robert Dumont)** works in the same area: a parameter-free Standard Model, Yang–Mills claims and
   Erdős–Straus claims. The same guidance applies.
