  [header] The Orion Chronicles
  The Orion Chronicles is a shared MMO world where players explore, develop characters, raise and use dragons, build communities, complete quests, master skills, and take part in competitive and educational activities. It is the game-world layer of the larger ORION experience, alongside the social Playground and the skill-development platform called The Ascent. 
  Your player journey
  You can approach the game in several ways rather than following one mandatory path:
  [bulleted_list] Explore the world and discover its systems.
  [bulleted_list] Create and develop a character.
  [bulleted_list] Choose a species, body form, and archetype.
  [bulleted_list] Learn skills and elemental abilities.
  [bulleted_list] Craft equipment, items, and schematics.
  [bulleted_list] Build and expand cities.
  [bulleted_list] Complete quests and training challenges.
  [bulleted_list] Capture, train, and utilize game entities.
  [bulleted_list] Bring trained assets into Polygon activities.
  [bulleted_list] Compete in the Polygon Arena.
  [bulleted_list] Advance through classrooms, lessons, and educational quests.
  [bulleted_list] Contribute to larger community projects.
  The overall experience is designed to let you move from discovery to learning, from learning to adventure, and from adventure to meaningful contribution. 
  [sub_header] Character creation and development
  Your character’s species, body, archetype, and skin serve different purposes:
  [bulleted_list] Species establishes your character’s underlying identity.
  [bulleted_list] Body changes can influence abilities.
  [bulleted_list] Archetypes provide specialized development paths.
  [bulleted_list] Skin is cosmetic and does not change ability behavior.
  [bulleted_list] Acquired traits and skills can expand what your character can do without replacing the character’s original species.
  The archetype system includes the developing relationships between Kronos, Orion, and KRION. These represent different styles of energy, play, and ability development. 
  [sub_header] Dragons
  Dragons are one of the game’s central forms of character development. Players can develop dragons, learn elemental abilities, and shape builds around different strengths and play styles.
  A dragon may:
  [bulleted_list] Learn elemental abilities.
  [bulleted_list] Develop skills through quests and travel.
  [bulleted_list] Gain traits associated with other creatures.
  [bulleted_list] Build toward different combat and exploration roles.
  [bulleted_list] Prepare new abilities outside combat.
  [bulleted_list] Use safe-zone meditation to permanently integrate skill changes.
  The method for generating a dragon is intentionally left for players to discover. The world is designed to provide clues and context without simply handing over the answer.
  Newly earned abilities do not become instantly usable in the middle of an encounter. They remain pending until the player returns to a safe zone and meditates. 
  [sub_header] Skills, progression, and meditation
  Progress can happen while traveling, questing, exploring, and playing. However, the game does not automatically force every newly earned improvement into your active build.
  Safe-zone meditation is the deliberate progression step that allows you to:
  [bulleted_list] Lock in improved skill strength.
  [bulleted_list] Add newly earned abilities.
  [bulleted_list] Integrate elemental changes.
  [bulleted_list] Prepare a build before returning to danger.
  [bulleted_list] Make meaningful decisions about how your character develops.
  This gives progression a strategic rhythm: adventure creates potential, while meditation turns that potential into dependable ability.
  [sub_header] Quests
  Quests are designed around what a player can reasonably learn and accomplish.
  The game supports:
  [bulleted_list] Standard quests.
  [bulleted_list] Leveled quests.
  [bulleted_list] Sub-leveled quests for different progression experiences.
  [bulleted_list] Educational quests.
  [bulleted_list] Epic training quests.
  [bulleted_list] Developer-created training content.
  [bulleted_list] Player-created training content.
  A fair quest should not require a skill that the player had no reasonable way to obtain before reaching that stage. Earlier stages should teach or provide what is needed for later stages.
  Epic training quests can remain locked until a player reaches the necessary skill level. Their purpose is to provide longer-term goals and structured preparation for advanced content. There are endless opportunities at all skill level, so no one is ever truly stuck.
  [sub_header] Crafting and city building
  Crafting is more than an item-production activity. It is part of how players shape the world.
  Players can expect crafting to support:
  [bulleted_list] Equipment and useful items.
  [bulleted_list] Schematics and repeatable designs.
  [bulleted_list] Resource gathering and material use.
  [bulleted_list] Contributions to personal and shared projects.
  [bulleted_list] Construction and city development.
  [bulleted_list] Economic activity through player-created goods.
  City building gives players a way to turn individual progress into something visible and communal. Instead of only improving a character, players can help create places where other players gather, learn, trade, prepare, and participate.
  Crafting does not automatically bypass every progression gate. Certain major areas and discoveries require quests, keys, or specific progression achievements rather than being reached solely by manufacturing a device. 
  [sub_header] The POLY Program
  The game includes a capture–train–utilize feature loop. Players can capture game entities, develop their capabilities, and then bring them into later activities rather than treating every encounter as a one-time battle.
  The intended flow is:
  [numbered_list] Discover or encounter something worth capturing.
  [numbered_list] Capture it.
  [numbered_list] Train or develop it.
  [numbered_list] Learn how it fits your play style.
  [numbered_list] Utilize it in later activities.
  [numbered_list] Take it into Polygon-based challenges or competitive play.
  This creates another layer of progression alongside ordinary character development.
  [sub_header] Polygon and the Arena
  The Polygon is a major destination for testing skill, preparation, and strategy. Players can bring trained assets into Polygon activities and use the space to demonstrate how well their builds perform.
  The Polygon Arena is intended to provide:
  [bulleted_list] Competitive encounters.
  [bulleted_list] Build testing.
  [bulleted_list] Strategic combat.
  [bulleted_list] Dragon and companion utilization.
  [bulleted_list] Player-versus-player or challenge-based experiences.
  [bulleted_list] A place where preparation matters as much as raw power.
  The Arena is not simply a place to press “fight.” Your choices about species, archetype, elements, training, timing, and meditation all influence how prepared you are when you enter.
  [sub_header] Combat and aggro
  Combat includes both direct encounters and environmental effects.
  Aggro is designed to belong to the player who creates it:
  [bulleted_list] Directly engaging an encounter can create aggro.
  [bulleted_list] Environmental actions can also create aggro.
  [bulleted_list] One player’s aggro does not automatically change another player’s timer.
  [bulleted_list] Teammates can create their own aggro through their own actions.
  [bulleted_list] The timer is local to the player.
  [bulleted_list] Leaving the skybox clears the player’s aggro.
  This supports both cooperative play and individual responsibility during group encounters. [^https://app.notion.com/p/3ee5aabf6a1180a6859cf8c4c0cb1f8a]
  [sub_header] Exploration and the skybox
  The skybox represents a higher exploration layer with its own access requirements.
  Players cannot simply craft a rocket or device and bypass the intended progression. Access depends on completing the relevant quest, using the appropriate escape feature, or obtaining a key.
  That makes reaching higher areas an achievement tied to exploration and discovery rather than merely having enough materials.
  [sub_header] The Ascent and endgame education
  The Orion Chronicles connects to The Ascent, an education and progression system designed to keep learning part of the game rather than treating it as something that ends when combat begins.
  The Ascent may include:
  [bulleted_list] Skill lines.
  [bulleted_list] Lessons.
  [bulleted_list] Tutorials.
  [bulleted_list] Tests.
  [bulleted_list] Technical training.
  [bulleted_list] Meditation education.
  [bulleted_list] Educational quests.
  [bulleted_list] Epic training quests.
  [bulleted_list] Classroom-style learning.
  [bulleted_list] Demonstrations of practical ability.
  Lessons can introduce the next stage of growth without pretending that the player can already perform it. The game can show you what you are working toward while keeping the actual action locked until you have earned it.
  At higher levels, classrooms and training systems become part of the endgame. Players can continue developing mastery, preparing for advanced challenges, and building the knowledge needed to participate in more demanding activities. 
  [sub_header] Community and the wider ORION experience
  The Playground provides the social and creative side of the experience:
  [bulleted_list] Meet other players.
  [bulleted_list] Share artwork and theories.
  [bulleted_list] Recruit collaborators.
  [bulleted_list] Discuss strategies.
  [bulleted_list] Develop ideas.
  [bulleted_list] Form groups and projects.
  The broader ORION ecosystem can then connect those social experiences with education, gameplay, and organized community projects. Players may enter through the social side, the educational side, or the MMO itself. [^https://app.notion.com/p/3ee5aabf6a1180158b33f913e85bee68]
  [header] Why play The Orion Chronicles?
  The game is built around more than a single combat loop. It combines:
  [bulleted_list] Exploration.
  [bulleted_list] Dragon development.
  [bulleted_list] Elemental strategy.
  [bulleted_list] Crafting.
  [bulleted_list] City building.
  [bulleted_list] Capture and training.
  [bulleted_list] Arena competition.
  [bulleted_list] Quest progression.
  [bulleted_list] Player-created content.
  [bulleted_list] Educational advancement.
  [bulleted_list] Social collaboration.
  [bulleted_list] Long-term discovery.
  The strongest appeal is that the game gives different kinds of players different ways to participate. You can focus on combat, crafting, cities, exploration, dragons, education, competition, community building, or a combination of all of them.
  The world is meant to reward curiosity. Some systems are explained directly; others are meant to be uncovered through play.
